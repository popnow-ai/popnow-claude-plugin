# UGC ad script

UGC ads look like a real person talking about a product on their phone. They work because they feel personal and specific. The script must sound spoken, not written.

## Inputs

- Product, price point and the one main benefit.
- Who the creator is talking to (age range, situation, pain point).
- Real features and proof the brand can stand behind.
- Length: 15, 30 or 60 seconds (default 30). Platform (default 9:16).
- Whether a real creator will film it, or it will be produced with AI video using an original, fictional presenter.

## Structure

| Beat | Time (30 s) | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0 to 3 s | Stop the scroll: a bold line or a surprising visual |
| Problem | 3 to 8 s | A specific, relatable moment |
| Product | 8 to 12 s | Introduce it naturally, show it in hand |
| Demo | 12 to 22 s | Show it working, 2 or 3 quick cuts |
| Proof | 22 to 26 s | A true detail: a feature, a result, a number |
| CTA | 26 to 30 s | One clear next step |

Scale the timings for 15 or 60 seconds.

## Writing rules

- Short sentences, contractions, first person. Read it aloud: about 2.5 spoken words per second in English, so a 30-second script is about 70 words.
- One idea per sentence. Cut adjectives that do not carry information.
- Never script fake personal experiences for a real creator to claim. If a real person will say "I've used this for a month", it must be true for them.
- If the presenter is AI-generated, make them an original fictional character and follow the platform's AI-content disclosure rules. Do not imitate a real creator or celebrity.
- Health, finance, beauty and supplement claims: stick to what the brand can substantiate and flag anything risky.

## Output

1. The script as a two-column table: spoken line | what we see.
2. A shot list (use the `shot-list` format) with phone-camera framing: selfie, over-the-shoulder, hand close-up, product on a surface.
3. Three alternative hooks (or use the `ad-hooks` skill for more).
4. Caption text and 3 to 5 hashtags.
